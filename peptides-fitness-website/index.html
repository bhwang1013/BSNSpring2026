Frequently Asked Questions

Peptides are short chains of amino acids (typically 2–50) that act as signaling molecules in the body. Unlike full proteins, their small size allows them to cross certain biological barriers and interact directly with receptors, making them highly targeted in their effects.

No. Peptides are structurally and mechanistically different from anabolic steroids. Most peptides work by stimulating your body's own hormone production rather than introducing synthetic hormones, resulting in a much more targeted and generally milder effect profile.

The legal status of peptides varies significantly by country and intended use. Some (like Semaglutide under brand names) are FDA-approved medications. Others are sold as "research chemicals" and are legal to purchase but not approved for human use. Always check your local laws and consult a physician.

Many peptides are administered via subcutaneous (under the skin) injection because oral bioavailability is poor — peptides can be broken down by stomach acid before absorption. However, some peptides like BPC-157 show effects orally, and others like Selank can be taken intranasally.

Potential risks include water retention, fatigue, increased hunger, injection site reactions, hormonal disruption, and unknown long-term effects for many compounds. Because many peptides are not fully studied in humans, caution is warranted. Always source from reputable labs and consult a healthcare provider.
